Reasoning · The Three Mandates

How Each Agent Thought

Three Claude-driven reasoning agents were spawned in parallel as a LangGraph orchestration. Each was given the S&P 500 history through April 2022 and instructed to produce a confidence-weighted ten-stock portfolio.

Mandate № I — The patient compounder.

Identify S&P 500 names with sustained upward momentum and consistent linear growth. Reward the stocks that climb without theatrics.

Strategy
Low-vol momentum
Final Return
+21.38%
Final Value

Methodology
  • Compute per-ticker daily returns, standard deviation, and a Sharpe-like ratio (mean ÷ std)
  • Fit a linear slope to the closing price across the 11-year window
  • Compare current close to historical mean (close-vs-mean %); a value above 100% confirms the trend is intact and not reverting
  • Filter out high-volatility candidates (std > 0.018); rank survivors by Sharpe-like ratio + slope
  • Allocate top 10 by confidence weight, summing to exactly 100%

Verdict

The book compounded steadily but two of the highest-conviction picks — POOL and WST — broke down on housing-cycle and biotech-capex weakness, dragging the strategy below the index. Discipline without surprise.

Mandate № II — Buy what the market just abandoned.

Identify quality names trading well below their historical mean with a credible mean-reversion thesis. The agent looks for dislocation, not decay.

Strategy
Mean reversion

Methodology
  • Compute close-vs-historical-mean discount; require ≥15% below long-run average
  • Filter for positive long-run total return — the company must have compounded historically, even if currently dislocated
  • Cross-check positive mean daily return — recent dip is a deviation, not a structural break
  • Tag dislocation cause: macro/rate-driven, COVID-driven, or geopolitical (Russia-Ukraine), and prefer macro-resolvable causes
  • Allocate by conviction; concentrate top picks more aggressively

Verdict

The clear winner — and by a wide margin. VRT (+2,485%) and PLTR (+1,017%) more than paid for the EPAM bet that never recovered from Russia-Ukraine exposure. Mean reversion compounded the most when the market's "abandonment" turned out to be a rate-cycle blip.

Methodology

  • Searches for analyst consensus ratings, earnings surprises, sector tailwinds, and any controversies / SEC actions / leadership changes
