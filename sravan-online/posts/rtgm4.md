# 90-Day Analysis: The Granular View

After examining six years of data, then one year, I wanted to push the temporal analysis even further. What do my most recent 90 days reveal? The answer: fascinating patterns, but also a crucial lesson about sample sizes and statistical significance.

## Recent Performance Snapshot

Over the past 90 days, I played **209 games** with a **+15 game surplus**—maintaining the positive momentum identified in my yearly analysis, though at a slightly reduced rate.

This represents about 2.3 games per day, showing consistent engagement with serious chess practice.

## Game Endings: The Resignation Puzzle Intensifies

**My Recent Wins:**
- 64% by resignation (2% above Chess.com average)
- 25% by checkmate (2% above average)

**My Recent Losses:**
- 83% by resignation (16% above average!)
- 14.9% by checkmate (5% below average)

The resignation discrepancy is even more pronounced in recent play. I'm winning by resignation at a normal rate, but losing by resignation far more often than typical players. This suggests two possibilities:

1. **I'm playing stronger opponents** who recognize winning positions more quickly
2. **I'm still resigning too early** in positions with fighting chances

The low checkmate loss rate (5% below average) supports the first theory—I'm facing players who don't need to chase checkmate to win.

## Opening Analysis: Extreme Results from Small Samples

**With White - Wild Variations:**

- **Italian Game:** +25% edge (solid performance)
- **Caro-Kann Defense:** +45% edge (massive improvement from lifetime struggles!)
- **Alapin Sicilian:** -15% edge (concerning reversal)
- **Petrov Defense:** -100% over 4 games (0-4 record, but tiny sample)

**With Black - Confirming Long-Term Patterns:**

- **Caro-Kann:** +18% edge (consistently strong)
- **Sicilian Defense:** -7% edge (persistent weakness)
- **Queen's Gambit:** -60% edge (alarming, but needs context on game count)
- **King's Indian:** +40% edge (excellent performance)

## The Sample Size Reality Check

Here's where this analysis hits a crucial limitation: **with only 209 games over 90 days, many opening-specific results are based on tiny samples.** 

The Petrov's -100% record sounds devastating, but it's only 4 games. The Queen's Gambit's -60% could be 2-3 bad games. Even the Caro-Kann's excellent results might represent just 8-10 games.

**Statistical truth:** You need at least 20-30 games in an opening to draw meaningful conclusions about your performance.

## What the Data Actually Tells Us

**Reliable Insights (sufficient sample sizes):**
- Overall positive performance continues (+15 games)
- Resignation patterns remain consistent with yearly data
- Caro-Kann strength persists across all time frames
- Sicilian weakness as Black remains problematic

**Interesting but Unconfirmed (small samples):**
- Dramatic Caro-Kann improvement as White
- Alapin Sicilian struggles
- Queen's Gambit difficulties
- King's Indian success

## The Temporal Analysis Conclusion

This 90-day dive demonstrates both the power and limits of data analysis:

**What temporal analysis revealed:**
- My dramatic improvement started exactly one year ago
- Recent play maintains positive momentum
- Core strengths and weaknesses persist across timeframes
- Opening preparation improvements are real and sustained

**What it couldn't reveal:**
- Specific tactical improvement (need puzzle/analysis data)
- Recent opening innovations (sample sizes too small)
- Opponent strength trends (need rating data)

## Final Recommendations Based on Complete Analysis

Combining all timeframes, my action plan is clear:

**Immediate Focus:**
1. **Stop playing the Sicilian with Black** - consistent weakness across all timeframes
2. **Develop Queen's Gambit repertoire** - recent struggles need attention
3. **Continue Caro-Kann with both colors** - reliable strength
4. **Study resignation timing** - 16% above average resignation rate needs addressing

**Study Priorities:**
1. French and Caro-Kann defenses (as White)
2. Alternative Black responses to 1.e4 (replace Sicilian)
3. Queen's Gambit declined structures
4. Endgame technique (to reduce premature resignations)

## Why I'm Stopping Here

With diminishing sample sizes making further temporal breakdown unreliable, this completes my data-driven analysis series. The key insight isn't in the granular 30-day or 7-day data—it's in understanding which patterns persist across multiple timeframes versus which are statistical noise.

**The meta-lesson:** Data analysis is powerful, but knowing when you have enough data is just as important as knowing how to analyze it.

In my next posts, I'll shift from analysis to action—implementing these insights through focused study and practice. Sometimes the best analysis is knowing when to stop analyzing and start improving.
