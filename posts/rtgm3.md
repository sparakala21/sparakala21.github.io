# Temporal Analysis: The Power of Recent Data

In my previous post, I analyzed six years of chess data and found some concerning patterns—particularly a devastating -17% performance with the Sicilian Defense. But here's where temporal analysis becomes crucial: those lifetime statistics were masking a dramatic recent transformation.

## The Stunning Revelation: A Complete Turnaround

My overall performance tells an incredible story of recent improvement:

**Lifetime (6 years):** +57 games above break-even  
**Past year alone:** +59 games above break-even

This means that from November 2019 to August 2024, I was actually **net negative** in my win-loss record. Everything changed in the last 12 months. This perfectly demonstrates why looking at recent data is essential—I was judging my current abilities based on years-old weaknesses.

## The Rating Mathematics

The numbers align beautifully with rating theory. My 59-game surplus over the past year translates to approximately 354 ELO points gained (59 × 6 ≈ 354). However, this wasn't pure linear progress—much of this gain recovered ELO lost in previous years, which explains why I want to focus on more mindful, consistent play rather than the boom-bust cycles of my past.

## Game Endings: Measurable Improvement

**Recent Performance (Past Year):**
- Win by resignation: 64% (down from 60% lifetime)
- Win by checkmate: 27% (down from 31% lifetime)
- Lose by resignation: 76% (improved from 78% lifetime)  
- Lose by checkmate: 21% (improved from 20% lifetime)

I'm still 9% more likely to resign than the average Chess.com player, but the trend is positive. I'm clearly developing better fighting spirit and game evaluation skills. The slight decrease in checkmate wins suggests I'm facing stronger opponents who recognize losing positions earlier.

**Progress indicator:** My resignation rate is moving in the right direction, showing improved resilience.

## Opening Analysis: Dramatic Shifts Emerge

**With White - Recent Dominance:**

The data reveals a complete reversal in several key areas:
- **Against Sicilian:** Previously struggled, now showing a remarkable **+33% win rate**
- **Against Caro-Kann & French:** Still challenging, confirming these remain genuine weaknesses
- **Against Petrov:** Struggling, but with only 12 games, this needs more data

This Sicilian turnaround is huge—it suggests my preparation and understanding of these positions has significantly improved over the past year.

**With Black - Mixed Results:**

- **Caro-Kann:** Maintaining solid positive performance
- **Sicilian Defense:** Still deeply problematic with opponents holding a **10% edge**

## The Sicilian Paradox

Here's the fascinating contradiction: I'm crushing the Sicilian with White (+33%) but getting crushed when playing it with Black (-10%). This suggests:

1. **As White:** My anti-Sicilian preparation is excellent
2. **As Black:** I'm not comfortable in Sicilian structures from the defending side
3. **Strategic insight:** I understand how to attack the Sicilian but not how to play it

**Immediate decision:** Stop playing the Sicilian with Black until I've studied the defensive concepts more thoroughly. My success against it with White should actually help me understand what Black needs to avoid.

## Key Insights from Temporal Analysis

**What Changed:**
- Overall game management improved dramatically
- White opening preparation became much stronger
- Resignation patterns became slightly more reasonable
- Consistent positive momentum over 12 months

**What Remained Consistent:**
- Struggles with Caro-Kann and French as White
- Sicilian weakness as Black
- Above-average resignation tendency

## The Mindful Play Connection

The data supports my goal of playing more mindfully. The past year's +59 game surplus came from sustained improvement rather than rating volatility. This suggests that when I focus and prepare properly, I can maintain consistent positive results.

## Next Steps: Targeted Improvement

Based on this temporal analysis, my priorities are clear:

1. **Study French and Caro-Kann defenses** - These remain consistent weaknesses with White
2. **Temporarily abandon the Sicilian with Black** - The 10% deficit is too costly
3. **Continue current preparation methods** - Whatever I've been doing this past year is working
4. **Maintain fighting spirit improvements** - The resignation trend is positive

## The Power of Recent Data

This analysis proves why temporal breakdowns are essential. My lifetime statistics suggested I was a struggling player with multiple opening weaknesses. The recent data reveals I'm actually in a strong improvement phase with specific, addressable gaps.
